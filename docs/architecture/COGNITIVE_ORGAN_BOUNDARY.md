# Hyperconnectome Cognitive-Organ Boundary

Status: template architecture invariant.

## Thesis

The Hyperconnectome Brain is a self-contained synthetic cognitive organ. A compatible body may supply sensors, actuators, energy, circulation/cooling, mechanical support, and external communications, but the functions that make an instantiated synthetic organism cognitively itself belong inside the HC boundary.

> **No essential cognition occurs outside the Hyperconnectome Brain.**

This is an architectural ownership rule, not a claim that every physical implementation must place every transistor or storage cell in one enclosure. A physically remote component may still be part of the HC only when it is architecturally inside the governed cognitive boundary: HC-owned, state-bound, provenance-bound, failure-accounted, and not an independent substitute for the organ's cognition.

## Essential cognition

Essential cognition includes at minimum:

- perception interpretation and multisensory integration;
- world modeling, prediction, reasoning, hypothesis formation, and learning;
- semantic and pragmatic interpretation;
- salience, attention, valuation, affective and homeostatic modulation;
- memory admission, consolidation, retrieval, currentness, chronology, and autobiographical continuity;
- self-modeling, self/other distinction, social cognition, empathy, sexuality, and personification capacities where present;
- conation, intention formation, goal competition, commitment, and decision formation;
- action selection and internal authorization;
- internal conflict resolution, arbitration, coalition formation, and state propagation;
- neuroplastic adaptation of the cognitive network itself.

No external language model, database, cloud service, retrieval engine, operator workstation, or peripheral is allowed to become the hidden place where these functions actually reside while the HC merely relays requests.

## Internal services versus external peripherals

A model, database, accelerator, or specialized inference engine may be used in either of two roles.

### HC-internal service

It counts as part of the cognitive organ when its state and behavior are governed as part of the HC architecture, its inputs/outputs participate in HC provenance and arbitration, its failure is represented as an internal cognitive fault, and replacing it does not silently replace the instantiated organism.

### External computational peripheral

It remains outside the cognitive organ when it is an optional service or external source. Its output enters the HC as typed evidence, measurement, recommendation, or computation result. The HC retains interpretation, confidence assignment, integration, memory admission, valuation, and action authority.

`EXTERNAL_OUTPUT != INTERNAL_BELIEF`

`EXTERNAL_RECOMMENDATION != HC_DECISION`

`MODEL_RESPONSE != ORGANISM_IDENTITY`

## Body independence

The HC owns the cognitive body-model and interface adaptation; the host body owns physical sensing and actuation hardware.

A compatible HC may therefore move between materially different embodiments without changing the definition of the brain itself. Humanoid limbs, wheels, manipulators, distributed cameras, microphones, drones, or remote actuators are body/interface configurations, not new cognitive organs.

Body replacement requires re-registration, calibration, capability discovery, body-schema adaptation, safety revalidation, and potentially new motor learning. It does not by itself imply identity replacement.

`BODY_CONFIGURATION != BRAIN_IDENTITY`

## Complete latent architecture

The template describes capacities the cognitive organ can contain, not only capacities enabled in the first implementation. Core subsystems should therefore have an architectural home even when a specific instantiated brain has them dormant, disabled, undeveloped, inhibited, degraded, or faulted.

Capacity absence is different from capacity inactivity.

## System classes

All classes remain inside the HC boundary.

### Intrinsic cognitive systems

Examples: cognition, memory, semantics, pragmatics, affect, empathy, sexuality, conation, self-modeling, personification, social/psychological behavior, learning.

### Interface systems

Examples: adaptable I/O, optics integration, speech recognition/synthesis, somatics/interoception, kinesis, network and body interfaces.

### Cross-cutting brain infrastructure

Examples: Noöplex Fabric, chronology, routing, salience/attention, integration/arbitration, resolver, homeostasis, provenance/currentness, fault handling, and neuroplasticity.

These are organizational classes, not hemispheres, lobes, or privileged control centers.

## No homunculus

The HC may contain integration infrastructure, but no module is a miniature person that all other systems consult.

The instantiated mind is the ongoing, governed, distributed activity of the whole organ across time. The center of an architecture diagram may represent the Noöplex / Hyperconnectome Fabric, never a hidden executive self.

## Template boundary

This document defines a reusable brain template. It does not define the memories, personality, values, biography, morphology, relationships, preferences, or self-concept of any named future implementation.
